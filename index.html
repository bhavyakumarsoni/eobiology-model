Triangle, square, and hexagon all tile a plane with zero gaps. Among them, hexagon is mathematically proven (Honeycomb Conjecture, Hales 1999) to need the least wall material per unit area.
Circles cannot tile
Packed circles always leave gaps between them — unlike triangle, square, and hexagon (the plane's only true regular tessellations), circles can never fully cover a flat area with zero gaps. The red patches below are exactly those uncovered gaps.
